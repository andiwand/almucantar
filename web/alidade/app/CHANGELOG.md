# Changelog

The changes in the PWA alidade that a user notices. Each release has the tag
`alidade-pwa-vX.Y.Z`. The rules are the same as in `core/CHANGELOG.md`.

## Unreleased

- The first version, a prototype of the photo planner. Every way ends in the shot: the photo from
  the spot in first person, with the real terrain, the landmark, the subject and its path, the
  stars, a time slider, and the frame of a lens, in the look of the website.
- "I have a landmark": the subject, the landmark on a map of the terrain, then "I know my spot"
  (the dates of the next 12 months as a contact sheet of photos, with a check of the view) or "Find
  me a spot" (a moment, then the spots on the map; a full dot has a clear view). Without a match,
  the app says if the subject never comes to that direction in the year.
- The eclipses: the next solar and lunar eclipses with what your place sees, the path of the
  shadow on the map of the Earth, the phases over the real terrain, and the eclipse in the shot:
  the moon in front of the sun, the corona at totality, or the red moon in the umbra.
- In the shot, drag to look around and pinch or scroll to zoom; the time is next to its slider.
- A high subject alone stays inside its small photo, also near the top.
- "From where I am" (the first way), now or on some days: the moon, the Milky Way, the sun, and the planets while
  they are over the real horizon of the place, and the peaks of the horizon that they pass.
- The hours on the path of the subject are the full hours of the spot, also in a time zone with an
  offset of 30 or 45 minutes.
- After a time offline, the map and the horizon load the missing terrain again.
- The map of an eclipse zooms out to the whole Earth, and the forward button of the browser keeps
  the screen.
- The shot follows a drag with less delay, and a failed search shows an error.
- A tap on a done step in the strip at the top goes back to that step.
- A thin crescent of the moon (3 to 10 % lit) is a shot when the sun is at least 3° below the
  horizon, in the twilight. A moon with less light is not a shot.
