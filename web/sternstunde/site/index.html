Sternstunde

The night sky over the real horizon.

Hold up your phone, and Sternstunde shows the stars, the constellations, the planets, and the Milky Way in that direction – over the mountains as they really are, and without a network.

01Dusk

The first stars

After sunset the sky gets dark slowly. First the planets and the brightest stars appear, then more and more. Sternstunde shows the sky as your eyes see it at that moment.

–

02Constellations

Lines between the stars

All 88 constellations, with their lines and names, and 5000 stars down to the faintest that the eye sees under a dark sky. Tap a star, and Sternstunde tells you its name.

–

03The night

Everything turns around one point

In the north, the sky turns around the celestial pole, next to Polaris. Stars rise in the east and set in the west. Sternstunde shows when each one rises and sets.

–

04Planets

Wanderers

The stars stay where they are, but the planets move against them, week by week. Here the sky is at the same sidereal time each night, so only the planets move.

–

05The real horizon

Behind the mountain

Most sky apps use a flat horizon at 0°. In the mountains, a star rises much later. Sternstunde knows the terrain around you, so its times of rise and set are the ones you see.

–

06The Milky Way

When the Milky Way is best

The Milky Way needs a dark sky: no sun, no moon, and its bright center high above the horizon. Sternstunde shows all three.

–

07The compass

Align with a star

The compass of a phone is often wrong by 10° or more, so the phone draws the stars in the wrong place. Tap one bright star or a planet, and Sternstunde puts the whole sky where it really is. It also shows how far it can trust the heading.

Precise where it matters

The stars come with their proper motion, the precession, the nutation, the aberration, and the refraction; the planets and the moon come from Astronomy Engine. A star on the screen is a few pixels large, so the astronomy is not the limit: the compass is, and Sternstunde shows how good it is.

Stars 5080 stars to magnitude 6, better than 0.01°
Planets within 0.006° of NASA's JPL Horizons
Terrain elevation data of 90 m within 100 km, offline for a kept place

Everywhere

Browser

The web app works offline after the first visit. On a computer, drag to look around and scroll to zoom.

Android

The native app, with the camera image, is in testing and not in Google Play yet.

iOS

The native app in SwiftUI, with the camera image. Build it from the repository; it is not in the App Store yet.
